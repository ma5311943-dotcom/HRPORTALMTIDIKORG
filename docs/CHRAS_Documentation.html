WHY THIS SYSTEM EXISTS
02  /  Purpose

The Problem and the Solution

Without a system

  • Candidate files live in registers, Excel sheets and paper folders.
  • Officers cannot quickly see who is verified, allocated or pending.
  • The same person can be appointed to two active roles by mistake.
  • Candidates have no clear way to check their own status.
  • Reports require manual counting and take hours to prepare.
  • There is no consistent record of who cancelled an appointment and why.

With CHRAS

  • Every candidate has one digital record from enrolment onward.
  • Dashboard cards and filters show verified, pending and allocated counts.
  • The system blocks a second active allocation for the same candidate.
  • Candidates log in and view their own seat and appointment letter.
  • Reports are generated from live data in a few clicks.
  • Revocation requires a written reason and automatically frees the seat.
Simple example: A candidate registers, an authorised officer verifies the record, an allocator assigns a seat, and the system creates the appointment reference. If the appointment is later cancelled, the reason is stored and the candidate returns to the verified pool.

Who uses the platform

A

Administration

Admin, MNA, MPA, District Chairperson, Civil Officer and Committee Head.

B

Field and office staff

UC Chairman, Councilor and Public Sector Staff handle enrolment and record entry.

C

Candidates

Citizens with a read-only portal for their own record, allocation and letter.

Design principles

Role-based access

A staff worker can register a candidate but cannot allocate a seat unless the role allows it.

One source of truth

Dashboard, reports, allocation and candidate portal all read the same stored records.

Documented decisions

Allocations carry a reference number; cancellations carry a reason and date.

Read-only candidate view

Candidates can see their own information but cannot change official records.

END-TO-END FLOW
04  /  Workflow

Complete End-to-End Flow

1

Apply or register

The candidate signs up online, or an authorised officer registers them. CNIC, education, photograph and portal password are saved. Candidate, Staff, UC Chairman, Councilor, MNA, MPA, District Chairperson, Civil Officer, Admin.

2

Check and verify the record

The enrolling officer checks identity and documents. Officer enrolment saves the record as Verified; self-signup starts as Registered. Registering roles above; Committee Head can review but not edit.

3

Allocate a seat or role

The allocator selects the candidate, district, constituency, role and tenure. The system blocks a second active seat for the same candidate. Admin, MNA, MPA, District Chairperson, Civil Officer.

4

Issue the appointment letter

The system creates an official appointment reference and Gazette-style letter, and marks the candidate Allocated. System; allocator can view the letter.

5

Candidate views the result

The candidate logs in to a read-only portal and sees their own record, allocated seat and appointment letter. Candidate only.

6

Cancel with a reason, if needed

The authorised officer writes a reason. The appointment becomes Revoked, the candidate returns to Verified, and the seat is freed. Admin, District Chairperson, Civil Officer.

7

Report and coordinate

Dashboards show quota use, verification progress and role/party breakdowns. Reports can be generated and staff can message each other. Authorised report roles and all officer roles.

Core records

  • Candidate: identity, contact, domicile, party, academics, documents, status and allocated role.
  • District: province, constituencies, total quota and allocated count.
  • Allocation: reference number, candidate, role, district, constituency, tenure, issuing authority and status.
  • Message: sender, role, district, subject, body, priority and read state.

Status values

  • Candidate: Registered, Verified, Allocated, Under Review.
  • Allocation: Active, Revoked, Completed.
  • Priority: Urgent, Official Notice, Routine, Inquiry.

Security and production readiness

Implemented now: candidate passwords are hashed with scrypt and salts; password hashes never leave the server; sessions are stored locally; duplicate active allocations are blocked; revocation requires a stored reason.
Before real government deployment: replace demo officer passwords with real authentication/SSO; add server-side session and role enforcement on every API; move from JSON file storage to PostgreSQL or SQL Server; connect real document storage and NADRA verification; add audit logs, backups, HTTPS and access monitoring.
Why this matters: the current build is complete as a working demo and internal review system. The items above are production hardening, not missing screens in the normal user flow.
Confirmation point: officer enrolment currently counts as verification; self-registered candidates start as Registered. If a separate second approval step is required, confirm it before final acceptance.
